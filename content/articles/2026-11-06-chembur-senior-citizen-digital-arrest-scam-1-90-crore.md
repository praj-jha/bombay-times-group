A **78-year-old retired Tata company employee from Chembur East** has lost **₹1.90 crore** to cyber criminals who posed as anti-terror officials and kept her under so-called "digital arrest" for weeks, the *Free Press Journal* reported. The Eastern Region Cyber Police of the Mumbai Police have registered an FIR under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita and the Information Technology Act.

## How the scam unfolded

According to the complaint, the woman, who lives with her 80-year-old husband, received a WhatsApp message on **October 6** from a woman who claimed her **Aadhaar had been misused for money laundering**. Soon after, a man in police uniform made a WhatsApp video call.

Over the following days, callers impersonated a series of officials — including a supposed senior officer of the **Anti-Terrorism Squad (ATS)** and a person posing as a police commissioner. They told her that her name had surfaced in a **terror-funding and money-laundering investigation** and that she had received money from a terror suspect.

The fraudsters:

- told her police were watching her colony and that she **must not leave home** without permission;
- demanded **hourly messages** about her whereabouts;
- made her install the encrypted app **Signal**;
- showed her a fake **"RBI letter"** to make the probe look genuine; and
- collected her bank details and instructed her to **sell shares and break fixed deposits**.

Under threat of arrest, she transferred the money in stages via **RTGS** to multiple accounts. The callers promised the "case" would be settled and the money returned within 10 days — and then demanded more.

## How it came to light

Her nephew, who lives in the United States, learned of the transfers and alerted a family friend in Mumbai, who called the **1930 cyber crime helpline** and filed an online complaint on **November 1**. Police are now tracing the beneficiary accounts, which were held with branches of private and public sector banks in other states.

## "No police agency arrests anyone on a video call"

There is **no such thing as a "digital arrest"** under Indian law. Police, the CBI, the ED, the ATS or the RBI will never question or detain anyone over a video call, ask them to stay on camera, or demand that money be moved to a "safe" or "verification" account.

If you receive such a call:

1. **Hang up** — do not stay on the call or share screens.
2. **Do not transfer money** or share OTPs, bank or Aadhaar details.
3. **Call 1930** immediately or report at **cybercrime.gov.in** — the faster the report, the better the chance of freezing the money.
4. Tell a family member. Fraudsters rely on isolating their victims.
