The Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation will extend **steel nets over suburban nullahs** across Mumbai by **May 2028** at an estimated cost of **₹300 crore**, in a bid to stop residents from dumping garbage into storm-water drains.

"The project is expected to cost around Rs 300 crore," Additional Municipal Commissioner (Projects) **Abhijeet Bangar** told the *Free Press Journal*, adding that the measure could cut the civic body's desilting expenditure by **nearly 50 per cent**. A civic official said dumping itself is expected to fall by about half.

## From a Bandra pilot to the whole city

The Storm Water Drains department first tried steel nets in **December 2023** at nullahs in **P&T Colony, Bandra West**. After encouraging results, the BMC extended them to other vulnerable stretches near slums and chawls, where officials say the nets have curbed dumping.

Despite annual pre-monsoon desilting, bulky waste — furniture, cupboards, even sofas — continues to be thrown into nullahs, particularly in slum pockets, choking the flow and raising the risk of flooding during heavy rain.

Mumbai has **314 major nullahs** stretching over about **300 km**, spread across the island city, the eastern and western suburbs and along the Mithi river.

## Corporators question desilting claims

The announcement came as corporators at the BMC's Standing Committee meeting questioned the civic body's claims of effective desilting.

**Yamini Jadhav** (Shiv Sena) flagged incomplete work at the **Racecourse nullah, Korba nullah** and the **Wadala Truck Terminus** stretch, alleging that contractors were building ramps for machinery while workers did the actual cleaning by hand. She demanded strict action against negligent contractors.

**Harish Bhandirge** (BJP) said several nullahs would be found overflowing if inspected the same day, and pointed to waterlogging in parts of the city during Diwali.
