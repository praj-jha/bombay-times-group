**Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC)** on Thursday reported a **20% rise in standalone net profit to ₹12,076 crore** for the second quarter ended **September 30, 2026**, compared with ₹10,053 crore in the same quarter last year, helped by higher renewal and net premium collections.

## Key numbers (Q2 FY27 vs Q2 FY26)

| Metric | Q2 FY27 | Q2 FY26 |
|---|---|---|
| Net profit | ₹12,076 crore | ₹10,053 crore |
| Total income | ₹2,62,739 crore | ₹2,39,614 crore |
| Net premium income | ₹1,47,018 crore | ₹1,26,479 crore |
| Renewal premium | ₹68,375 crore | ₹64,996 crore |
| First-year premium | ₹10,432 crore | ₹10,836 crore |

For the **six months to September**, LIC's **value of new business (VNB)** — a key measure of the profitability of new policies — rose about **34% to ₹6,846 crore** from ₹5,111 crore. The **net VNB margin improved by 470 basis points to 22.3%**, from 17.6% a year earlier.

## Why first-year premium dipped

The one weak spot was **first-year premium**, which fell slightly. LIC CEO and Managing Director **R Doraiswamy** attributed the dip to a slowdown in **unit-linked insurance plan (ULIP)** purchases, which tend to fall when equity markets are weak, adding that "one should be buying at every dip".

He also said the insurer's focus on **higher ticket sizes** could lift the VNB margin further in the third and fourth quarters.

## Persistency mixed

Persistency — the share of policyholders who keep paying premiums — was mixed for the half-year. On a premium basis, the **13th-month persistency ratio slipped to 74.69%** from 75.29%, and the **61st-month ratio fell to 61.03%** from 63.81%. On a policy-count basis, 13th-month persistency improved, while 61st-month persistency declined.

LIC's shares will react to the numbers when markets open on Friday.
